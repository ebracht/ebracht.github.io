## Current Research Projects

# Does reaching reveal conflict in children’s fairness decision-making?

While there is a well-established gap between children’s early-emerging 
understanding of fairness and their later-emerging fairness behavior, *how*
children’s decision-making changes during this developmental period is less well
known. We aim to investigate the mechanisms that contribute to and the degree 
of conflict that may underlie these decisions by combining decision outcomes 
with measures of the decision-making process (e.g., reaction and movement time 
and reach trajectory measures) to gain a fuller picture of how and why children 
make the decisions they do.

*with Dr. Katie McAuliffe and Dr. Craig Chapman*


# Do costly signaling opportunities affect children’s third-party punishment?

Both adults and children engage in costly third-party punishment (TPP) of
unequal resource distributions, highlighting a willingness to take a normative 
stance against unfair behavior; however, the motivations underlying TPP–
particularly in children–remain poorly understood. This project explores whether
the presence of an observer increases children’s willingness to pay a cost to
prevent unfair resource distributions. We also look at how children engage in
costly TPP when they first have the opportunity to help, given that the adult 
literature indicates that people deferring to helping, a more directly
pro-social behavior, as a stronger signal when it’s available.

*with Dr. Katie McAuliffe and Dr. Paul Deutchman*


# A cognitive load manipulation for children

Cognitive load manipulations are useful tools for differentiating the cognitive 
processes that are more effortful from those that are more automatic. This 
distinction has been widely explored in adults and informs our understanding of 
topics like impression and stereotype formation, spatial-navigation processes, 
quantitative reasoning, and the nature of trust and cooperation. We followed up
the formation of a child-friendly load manipulation---the creation and 
validation of which is important given that children are still actively 
acquiring and refining many skills these manipulations often test---to determine
whether initial results were an effect of load or merely auditory distraction.

*with Dr. Katie McAuliffe and Dr. Yarrow Dunham*


# How and when do children internalize moral norms?

We are measuring participants' third-party evaluatations of others who have 
made decisions for external reasons or for internal reasons to---in addition to 
understanding when and how one's beahvior changes to reflect this 
internalization---explore how children and adults judge others’ actions
based on internal and external motivations. 

*with Dr. Katie McAuliffe*
